How it works

Same file. Two histories.

Two students hand in the exact same file. Here is what each one’s recording shows.

A history of connected events.

Each recorded event is linked to the one before it, forming a history that can be checked for changes.

Changes leave a trace.

Alter an earlier event, and the hashes that follow no longer match the original record.

Checkpoints protect the record.

Even if the chain is rebuilt, a signed checkpoint provides another way to detect changes that cannot be authenticated with the original signing key.

trails.slog Chain verified.

    Built not to accuse.

    Provenance points a person at the moments worth a look. Honest work stays out of the way.

    Safeguards

    Moving your own code isn’t copying.

    A paste that matches code you already typed is recognized as a move. It drops out of the review queue, and stays on the record.

    high Paste of 6 lines into trails.py

    Missing evidence is never evidence.

    A check that can’t run reports that it was skipped. It never reports a failure it couldn’t see.

    chain intact seal valid checkpoints: skipped

    Your course sets the rules.

    Staff can weight or switch off every signal, and a course can turn some kinds of capture off before anything is recorded.

    A person decides.

    Every flag opens the exact moment in the replay. Nothing happens automatically.

    A flag is a question, not a verdict.

    Recorded

    • Edits and pastes in the assignment folder
    • Saves, opened files and editor focus
    • Commands run in the editor’s terminal
    • Which editor extensions are installed

    Never recorded

    • Your browser, screen, camera or other projects
    • Keystrokes at the system level
    • Your clipboard, unless you paste into the work
    • Works offline No network requests while you work.
    • Readable local logs The log is plain text, on your computer until you hand it in.
    • Course-defined retention Stored logs are deleted on a schedule your school sets.

    For instructors & TAs

    The finished file is the easiest part to fake.

    Similarity checkers compare final submissions, and AI tools now produce final submissions on demand. What’s much harder to produce is the afternoon behind one: the false starts, the deleted lines, the test that finally passed at 11:40 p.m.

    Demo dynamic

    Grade the work. See the process.

    The replay shows where a student got stuck, what they tried, and when it clicked. Office hours can start from the actual evening, not a guess about it.

    Tools

    One record. Every tool.

    Three editors write the same signed log. Everything downstream reads it. Every piece is open source.

    Record

    provcode Provenance for VS Code Record development activity directly in VS Code. provjet Provenance for JetBrains Record development activity in supported JetBrains IDEs. provnvim Provenance for Neovim A lightweight Lua recorder for Neovim.

    Hand in

    The signed bundle The recorded history and its signed evidence, packaged for submission.
    provgate Gradescope gateway Transfers new Gradescope submissions to the Provenance server.

    Check

    provd Provenance server Receives submissions, verifies recorded histories, and stores the evidence for review.

    Review

    provlens Provenance analyzer Explore recorded activity, inspect flagged moments, and replay the development history.

      What it can’t do.

      No log is impossible to forge, and our own design documents say so. Provenance makes the easy shortcuts leave marks, and the hard ones cost more than doing the work. The source is public, and the design assumes it.

      “We will know if you do something obvious, and we will know enough to ask questions if you do something subtle.”
